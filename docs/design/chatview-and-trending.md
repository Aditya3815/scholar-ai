# ChatView and Home: speed, look, and live trending topics (research and plan, 3 Nov 2026)

Asked by the owner on 3 Nov 2026: make the chat page (ChatView, and the Home screen under it) **look better and be production grade**, tested well, and make the suggestion chips include **hot, trending topics that are always dynamic**, never a hand-written list.
Five read-only studies (four on the web with live API probes and a measured benchmark, one on this codebase) back this note. It follows [chat-actions-context.md](chat-actions-context.md), which changes the same files and is built first. Method: [how-we-work.md](../how-we-work.md).

## 1. What was measured (not guessed)

**Trending, with live probes of the open APIs (3 Nov 2026):**
- **No source has a "trending" feed** that is usable: OpenAlex has no trending sort (HTTP 400) and no time series below a year; Papers with Code redirects to Hugging Face; Crossref Event Data was shut down (23 Apr 2026); Altmetric and PlumX need keys; Semantic Scholar without a key answered 429 to every request; Google Trends is alpha by application; PIB, DST and ICMR feeds are blocked or not RSS.
- **"Last 30 days sorted by citations" is noise.** 9 of the 10 raw top results were journal front-matter pages; in the cleaned list about 1 of 10 was a genuinely new, hot paper, the rest were old papers re-registered with a 2026 date. **"Topics that grew most month over month" gave 0 of 10** (bulk repository dumps: OSF 1,034 of 1,305 works, Zenodo 2,484 of 2,577) and the list changed completely when the window moved by 45 days.
- **What works:** growth of **keyword phrases** in **journal articles** against the same dates a year earlier (OpenAlex), with quality filters, a share cap per source, and confirmation from arXiv's own volume for CS and physics. Examples that a researcher would call current: "retrieval-augmented generation" 452 papers in a quarter against 176, "world model" on arXiv 93 to 416 submissions per 30 days, "AI governance". Costs about 150 to 500 OpenAlex calls a week (under $0.05).
- The newest month is under-indexed by about 20% (counts of the last window look like a decline), journals stamp the 1st of the month, OpenAlex's own "retracted" flag marks 11 of 558,759 articles of Oct 2026 against 610 of 413,833 of Oct 2021, so **"exclude retracted" is nearly blind for fresh papers**. Topic names are not search phrases ("Topic Modeling" for RAG; "Cold Fusion" under Earth Sciences).
- Week-to-week overlap of a rising list is Jaccard 0.82 (4 weeks apart 0.43 to 0.82): a daily change needs hysteresis or it flickers.

**ChatView, measured in a harness shaped like our page** (20 turns of 20 paper cards, 19,291 DOM nodes, Edge 156, 4x CPU throttle; ordering stable across runs, absolute numbers not a prediction):
- Streaming one reply re-renders **every finished turn on every frame**: 4 fps and an INP-like delay of 528 ms. `memo` on finished turns with stable props: 14 fps, 120 ms. Adding `content-visibility: auto` on older turns (block-size longhand, not the one-value shorthand, which widened our grid to 5,200 px): about 31 fps, 64 to 72 ms, mount 1.6 s to 0.9 s.
- `layout="position"` on the result cards is the expensive animation (18 against 29 fps while finished).
- Per-word animated spans for the streamed answer: CLS 1.6 to 11.5 during a stream against 0.14 to 0.35 for one text node and 0.01 to 0.02 with space reserved. `perf.mjs` gates CLS only on first screens, so this is unguarded.
- One 31-character word (a chemical name) widened the whole conversation to 371 px at 320 and 360 px (`.chat-list` has no `minmax(0, 1fr)`; titles and abstracts have no `overflow-wrap`).
- Fixed `lang="en"` with no per-message `lang` or `dir`; Noto Serif Devanagari named in `--font-serif` but not bundled; three live regions per turn (about 60 in a 20-turn chat); no forced-colours or print rules for the chat.

**The Home page itself** (read from the code and the existing screenshots): the starters are the last thing on the page (one hard-coded array of four English phrases, no Hinglish although the design note promised two); the chips under a small-talk reply **ignore the Indian-sources scope and the busy state**; the cumulative layout shift has only 0.018 of headroom on desktop; first-load script is at **248.2 of 250 KB** after the chat release (about 5 KB each from message actions and from chat-to-chat), so nothing can be added before something is taken out.

## 2. Decisions

**Owner's answers, 3 Nov 2026:** the row is called **"Trending"** with a visible definition; a trending chip **fills the box** (the person edits and presses search; the ordinary "Try" chips keep sending); **health topics stay out at launch**. Owner's rule: trending is **always live**, never a list someone writes.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | A row **"Trending in research"** with a "Why these?" button (`aria-expanded`, not a tooltip) that says in plain words: "Topics with more journal papers than a year ago (and, for computing and physics, more submissions to arXiv this month). Source: OpenAlex and arXiv, updated <date>. The same list for everyone: we do not use what you searched. More papers is not better papers: nothing here is checked until you search." | There is no official definition of "trending"; the word is kept (owner) and made true by stating the measure. |
| T2 | **Nothing is hand-written.** A scheduled job builds the list from open data. The bundled "Try" chips (evergreen, owner-edited, two of them, one Hinglish, one India context) are a different row with a different label ("Try") and are never called trending. | Owner's rule; honest labels. |
| T3 | **Weekly candidates, daily refresh, deterministic daily pick.** Weekly (Monday inside the existing 03:17 UTC job): the OpenAlex keyword growth, filtered and verified, into a pool of about 40 phrases. Daily: re-score the pool's computing and physics phrases from arXiv (last 30 days against the 30 before and the same dates a year earlier), apply hysteresis (a shown phrase leaves only when a candidate scores 1.25 times higher or it drops out of the top 30), and publish one JSON. The page picks its chips from the top 12 by a seed of the `asOf` date, so **the row differs from day to day** without a timer and never reshuffles while someone types. | A rising list really changes weekly; a daily pick and an arXiv freshness check make the row move every day without flicker. |
| T4 | **Staleness is never shown as trending.** The payload carries `validUntil` (generated plus 48 h). After it, or when the pool is older than 14 days, or on `disabled`, the page shows **no Trending row at all** and keeps only "Try". The cron refuses to publish fewer than 8 verified phrases and leaves the last good file untouched on any source error. | Owner's rule. |
| T5 | **Signal:** OpenAlex keyword counts in window A (91 days ending 45 days ago, because the last month is under-indexed) against window B (the same dates 364 days earlier), journal articles with abstracts and fewer than 60 authors, not retracted, not paratext; score = the Wilson lower bound of `a/(a+b)` against the subfield's own growth; floors: 40 papers in A (150 for the global list, 30 for India); the phrase must not be in the top 10 of two or more subfields; at least 8 journals and the top journal at most 25%; at most 1 phrase per OpenAlex field and 2 per domain; an owner deny-list in KV. A phrase is shown only if it is **confirmed**: growth of at least 1.3 times in a second independent source (arXiv volume for computing, physics, maths) **or**, with no second source, a score floor of 1.5 and the journal checks above. | Every simpler method failed the live test. |
| T6 | **Health is out at launch.** The OpenAlex domain "Health Sciences" and its clinical subfields are `hold`: candidates are generated and logged but never published. A reviewed `subfield-policy` table (allow, hold, never for all 252 subfields) lives in the repository; the owner reads it once. | Owner's answer; the retracted flag cannot protect fresh content, so the control is the topic, not the paper. |
| T7 | **A chip is a search phrase, not a fact.** Its text is exactly the OpenAlex keyword's display name (at most 36 characters, no digits-only text, no URL), never written or reworded by a model, never taken from a title or abstract; each chip carries its two counts (shown in the Why panel and as its accessible description). | The product rule: a model never writes anything shown as fact. |
| T8 | **Verified before it is published**, with our own search (all providers, the same merge, and the Indian scope for India phrases): at least 20 results, 10 with abstracts, 5 from the last 24 months, none retracted in the top 20, no venue above 50%. A phrase that fails is replaced by the next candidate. | OpenAlex alone would drop good phrases (tirzepatide had 2 of 25 abstracts there) and nothing at all would show empty searches. |
| T9 | **Clicking fills the box** with the phrase, puts the cursor in it (on a phone it scrolls the box into view but does not force the keyboard until a real-phone test says so), and shows a small chip "From 2025 on" with a remove button (**`sinceYear`**, a number, set only by a Trending chip, sent as an optional body field, applied like the existing year filter before the cards are numbered, shown on the reply like the year chip). | A phrase with no date returns old papers (median year 2003 to 2020 for 4 of 10 tested): a Trending chip must mean recent. The filter is visible and removable, so "searched as written" stays true. |
| T10 | **India:** the payload has a `global` and an `in` group (authorships.institutions.country_code IN, floors of 30); the page shows the `in` group first when the Indian-sources switch is on. The data chooses, not an editorial list; evergreen India items (NEP 2020) live in "Try". Chip text stays English because the indexes are; only the labels may be translated. | No invented editorial line. |
| T11 | **No click counters, no personalisation in v1.** The feed is one public file, the same for everyone; the browser calls only our own API (never OpenAlex or arXiv: it would leak the visitor's address and CSP forbids it). Counting what is chosen would add a new thing kept: it waits for the owner's word. | Smallest privacy surface; the owner has not asked for counters. |
| T12 | **Serving:** KV key `trending:v1` (public data only), `GET /api/v1/home/trending` (not under `/search` or `/chat`, so it is outside their 60-a-minute bucket), `Cache-Control: public, max-age=300`, empty-but-valid answer on a KV miss (never calls OpenAlex on the request path), not in `sw.js`. Kill switch: a KV flag `trending:v1:off` (survives deploys, effective within about a minute) then the variable `TRENDING=off` (also in `wrangler.jsonc`). The page fetches after first paint (idle, 2.5 s timeout, silent on failure), keeps the answer for the **next** Home mount and never swaps chips under the reader; a device copy older than 24 h is ignored. The job is registered in `/api/v1/health/jobs` (with a seeded `job_runs` row in a migration, or `/health/jobs` answers 503) and `ops-e2e.mjs`'s expected job count is updated. | Patterns the repository already uses (cron heartbeat, KV cache, kill switch like `CHAT_JUDGE`). |
| C1 | **ChatView speed** (measured order of benefit): memoise finished turns and their cards with stable props; `content-visibility: auto` on all but the last two turns (block-size longhand, a measured median height with a phone value); the streamed answer as one text node (or one per sentence) with reserved space (no per-word spans); `layout="position"` only while a reply works; the long-token overflow fix; one shared live-region announcer instead of three or more per turn. **Not done on purpose:** list virtualisation (breaks find-in-page and scroll-to-card, not needed after the first two), a stick-to-bottom library (the page pins the question at the top and never follows the stream), card-level `content-visibility` (shifted in 46 of 58 scroll steps). | Each has a number from the harness. |
| C2 | **A chat performance gate** in the browser suite (`e2e/chat-perf.mjs`): a stored 20-by-20 chat, the stand-in model streaming one reply at 4x CPU, Event Timing at 16 ms, long tasks, layout shift for the whole stream, and a scroll-up probe through skipped turns. Starting lines (set after the first real run): mount at most 1.2 s, INP-like at most 200 ms, longest task after mount at most 150 ms, streaming CLS at most 0.1. | The wins must not regress silently; today nothing measures a long chat. |
| C3 | **Bundle first.** The chat release left 1.8 KB of script. Before anything is added the first-load script is measured by module and something is taken out (candidates: `ChatView` and the memory UI as a lazy chunk fetched on the first message or a resumed chat, Settings, the command palette, the stop-word lists, `route.ts` cue lists that only the server needs). The limit is **not** raised without the owner. | A feature that does not fit the budget does not ship. |
| C4 | **The Home page:** the chips move up under the box on a phone (the starters are last today); "Try" gets the two Hinglish and India chips the design note promised; the chips under a small-talk reply pass the Indian-sources scope and respect the busy state; a finished search with no papers says so plainly and offers the chips; the repeated "Auto picks the tool" copy is cut to one line; the chat title and New chat are reachable on a phone; the sticky box gets a soft fade; DOAJ, OpenAlex and arXiv are in the footer attribution (arXiv's terms ask for their acknowledgement line: checked against their API terms before the first call). | Weak spots found in the page, not taste. |
| C5 | **Script and language:** `lang="hi"` and `dir="auto"` per message when Devanagari letters dominate (Hinglish in Latin letters stays `en`); Noto Serif Devanagari bundled with a `unicode-range` subset (loaded only when needed) or a deliberate fallback; line-height of at least 1.45 where Devanagari can be clipped; minimal forced-colours and print rules for the chat (the cite flash and the composer focus ring are box-shadow only and vanish in forced colours). | Hindi and Hinglish are first-class; WCAG 3.1.2 and 1.4.10. |
| C6 | **How it looks.** A modest, measured polish, not a redesign: the reading column and spacing rhythm, the answer card, paper-card density, the empty state with the two chip rows, and the new action rows, judged against [UI-direction.md](UI-direction.md) and checked in light, dark and read themes and on a phone from real screenshots **before and after**, which the owner sees before anything is pushed. | The owner approved the current look; the change must be visible and safe. |

## 3. Tests (first)

- **Pure, in `packages/shared`, tested from `apps/api/src`:** the ranker and filters (small counts, a missing baseline, source concentration rejected, generic words rejected, a phrase in two subfields rejected, the diversity caps, hysteresis, the Wilson bound at the floors), the verifier thresholds, the chip text rules (36 characters, no URL, no digits-only, not small talk, passes `cleanChatMessage`), the date-seeded pick (same seed same chips; changes next day; not changed by 50 re-renders), `validUntil` and staleness, the kill switch, an empty KV, a throwing KV.
- **Server:** the cron against recorded OpenAlex and arXiv fixtures with an injected `fetch` (the `retraction-e2e.mjs` pattern with the stand-in behind `QA_STUB`), refusing to publish under 8 phrases, leaving the last file on an error, an arXiv "Error" Atom entry (HTTP 200) treated as an error, the route's headers, the `job_runs` row, `scale.test.ts` for any new query.
- **Contract:** every phrase in the latest snapshot and every bundled "Try" chip passes the verifier against a recorded search fixture; a weekly **live** job (like `eval-live.yml`) re-verifies the published file and the bundled chips and fails on an empty or retracted result; a hand-run `trending-live.mjs` shows the real list for a person to read.
- **Browser:** the row with the network blocked (evergreen only, no Trending), a stale payload ignored, `disabled:true`, a 3 s delayed answer that changes nothing on screen, fill puts the exact text in the box and sends nothing, `sinceYear` chip shown and removable, the request body for a filled and edited chip, axe in light, dark and read at 1280 and 390 px, 24 px minimum and 36 px measured targets, reduced motion, keyboard order (box, Try, Trending, Why), accessible names start with their visible text, only same-origin requests, layout shift under 0.1 with a delayed feed, and the chat performance gate above.
- **Human:** a 100-phrase sample per refresh judged by two raters ("would a researcher in this subfield call this current right now?", "is this a sensible search?"), precision at 4 of at least 0.8, kappa at least 0.6, week-to-week Jaccard at least 0.7. The numbers are the owner's to read on the first run; none has been measured yet.

## 4. Order of work

1. After the chat release is verified and pushed. 2. **Bundle first** (C3), then ChatView speed (C1) and its gate (C2) in the browser, **in parallel with** the trending server (T3 to T12 server half: pure ranker, fixtures, cron, KV, route, kill switch, `sinceYear` on the chat route). 3. The Home page and the Trending row (T1, T9, T10, C4) on top of both. 4. Script, language and look (C5, C6) with before and after screenshots to the owner. 5. A review by independent lenses and the full verification, as for the chat release. Rollback of the feed: the KV flag.

## 5. Not done, on purpose

- Hugging Face Daily Papers as a source (its terms are silent on automated use and its "trending" is star-driven; arXiv gives the same signal without the question), Semantic Scholar (no key), Wikipedia and Google Trends (celebrity spikes), social attention of any kind, Altmetric, press feeds.
- Click counters, a field picker, ordering chips from the person's local chats (v2, only with the owner's word and behind the existing off-by-default memory switch).
- A model that rewrites, translates or explains a chip.
- Health topics (T6), until the owner has a review process for them.
- Real-phone and screen-reader checks: not done by any study; the keyboard-on-phone behaviour of a filled chip and the on-screen keyboard against the sticky box need a real device.

## 6. Open for the owner

Whether to count chip use (aggregate only), whether a day's change is wanted or a weekly one is enough, the wording of the Why panel, the first human read of the generated list, a lawyer's read of the one new privacy sentence ("our server asks OpenAlex and arXiv once a day for public statistics; nothing about you is sent"), and when health topics may join.
