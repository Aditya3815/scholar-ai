# ChatView and Home: speed, look, and live trending topics (research and plan, 3 Nov 2026)

Asked by the owner on 3 Nov 2026: make the chat page (ChatView, and the Home screen under it) **look better and be production grade**, tested well, and make the suggestion chips include **hot, trending topics that are always dynamic**, never a hand-written list.
Five read-only studies (four on the web with live API probes and a measured benchmark, one on this codebase) back this note. It follows [chat-actions-context.md](chat-actions-context.md), which changes the same files and was built first (live since 3 Nov, 60de942). **Status (4 Nov 2026): everything in this plan except what section 5 and section 13.1 (item 12) list is built in the working tree, not committed and not live; the Trending job is committed off (4 Nov evening, section 13.7). Sections 7 to 13 say what was built and what differs from the plan, and section 13 is the one-page summary with the final numbers.** Method: [how-we-work.md](../how-we-work.md).

## 1. What was measured (not guessed)

**Trending, with live probes of the open APIs (3 Nov 2026):**
- **No source has a "trending" feed** that is usable: OpenAlex has no trending sort (HTTP 400) and no time series below a year; Papers with Code redirects to Hugging Face; Crossref Event Data was shut down (23 Apr 2026); Altmetric and PlumX need keys; Semantic Scholar without a key answered 429 to every request; Google Trends is alpha by application; PIB, DST and ICMR feeds are blocked or not RSS.
- **"Last 30 days sorted by citations" is noise.** 9 of the 10 raw top results were journal front-matter pages; in the cleaned list about 1 of 10 was a genuinely new, hot paper, the rest were old papers re-registered with a 2026 date. **"Topics that grew most month over month" gave 0 of 10** (bulk repository dumps: OSF 1,034 of 1,305 works, Zenodo 2,484 of 2,577) and the list changed completely when the window moved by 45 days.
- **What works:** growth of **keyword phrases** in **journal articles** against the same dates a year earlier (OpenAlex), with quality filters, a share cap per source, and confirmation from arXiv's own volume for CS and physics. Examples that a researcher would call current: "retrieval-augmented generation" 452 papers in a quarter against 176, "world model" on arXiv 93 to 416 submissions per 30 days, "AI governance". Costs about 150 to 500 OpenAlex calls a week (under $0.05).
- The newest month is under-indexed by about 20% (counts of the last window look like a decline), journals stamp the 1st of the month, OpenAlex's own "retracted" flag marks 11 of 558,759 articles of Oct 2026 against 610 of 413,833 of Oct 2021, so **"exclude retracted" is nearly blind for fresh papers**. Topic names are not search phrases ("Topic Modeling" for RAG; "Cold Fusion" under Earth Sciences).
- Week-to-week overlap of a rising list is Jaccard 0.82 (4 weeks apart 0.43 to 0.82): a daily change needs hysteresis or it flickers.

**ChatView, measured in a harness shaped like our page** (20 turns of 20 paper cards, 19,291 DOM nodes, Edge 156, 4x CPU throttle; ordering stable across runs, absolute numbers not a prediction):
- Streaming one reply re-renders **every finished turn on every frame**: 4 fps and an INP-like delay of 528 ms. `memo` on finished turns with stable props: 14 fps, 120 ms. Adding `content-visibility: auto` on older turns (block-size longhand, not the one-value shorthand, which widened our grid to 5,200 px): about 31 fps, 64 to 72 ms, mount 1.6 s to 0.9 s.
- `layout="position"` on the result cards is the expensive animation (18 against 29 fps while finished).
- Per-word animated spans for the streamed answer: CLS 1.6 to 11.5 during a stream against 0.14 to 0.35 for one text node and 0.01 to 0.02 with space reserved. `perf.mjs` gates CLS only on first screens, so this is unguarded.
- One 31-character word (a chemical name) widened the whole conversation to 371 px at 320 and 360 px (`.chat-list` has no `minmax(0, 1fr)`; titles and abstracts have no `overflow-wrap`).
- Fixed `lang="en"` with no per-message `lang` or `dir`; Noto Serif Devanagari named in `--font-serif` but not bundled; three live regions per turn (about 60 in a 20-turn chat); no forced-colours or print rules for the chat.

**The Home page itself** (read from the code and the existing screenshots): the starters are the last thing on the page (one hard-coded array of four English phrases, no Hinglish although the design note promised two); the chips under a small-talk reply **ignore the Indian-sources scope and the busy state**; the cumulative layout shift has only 0.018 of headroom on desktop; first-load script is at **248.2 of 250 KB** after the chat release (about 5 KB each from message actions and from chat-to-chat), so nothing can be added before something is taken out.

## 2. Decisions

**Owner's answers, 3 Nov 2026:** the row is called **"Trending"** with a visible definition; a trending chip **fills the box** (the person edits and presses search; the ordinary "Try" chips keep sending); **health topics stay out at launch**. Owner's rule: trending is **always live**, never a list someone writes.

## 3. Tests (first)

- **Pure, in `packages/shared`, tested from `apps/api/src`:** the ranker and filters (small counts, a missing baseline, source concentration rejected, generic words rejected, a phrase in two subfields rejected, the diversity caps, hysteresis, the Wilson bound at the floors), the verifier thresholds, the chip text rules (36 characters, no URL, no digits-only, not small talk, passes `cleanChatMessage`), the date-seeded pick (same seed same chips; changes next day; not changed by 50 re-renders), `validUntil` and staleness, the kill switch, an empty KV, a throwing KV.
- **Server:** the cron against recorded OpenAlex and arXiv fixtures with an injected `fetch` (the `retraction-e2e.mjs` pattern with the stand-in behind `QA_STUB`), refusing to publish under 8 phrases, leaving the last file on an error, an arXiv "Error" Atom entry (HTTP 200) treated as an error, the route's headers, the `job_runs` row, `scale.test.ts` for any new query.
- **Contract:** every phrase in the latest snapshot and every bundled "Try" chip passes the verifier against a recorded search fixture; a weekly **live** job (like `eval-live.yml`) re-verifies the published file and the bundled chips and fails on an empty or retracted result; a hand-run `trending-live.mjs` shows the real list for a person to read.
- **Browser:** the row with the network blocked (evergreen only, no Trending), a stale payload ignored, `disabled:true`, a 3 s delayed answer that changes nothing on screen, fill puts the exact text in the box and sends nothing, `sinceYear` chip shown and removable, the request body for a filled and edited chip, axe in light, dark and read at 1280 and 390 px, 24 px minimum and 36 px measured targets, reduced motion, keyboard order (box, Try, Trending, Why), accessible names start with their visible text, only same-origin requests, layout shift under 0.1 with a delayed feed, and the chat performance gate above.
- **Human:** a 100-phrase sample per refresh judged by two raters ("would a researcher in this subfield call this current right now?", "is this a sensible search?"), precision at 4 of at least 0.8, kappa at least 0.6, week-to-week Jaccard at least 0.7. The numbers are the owner's to read on the first run; none has been measured yet.

## 5. Not done, on purpose

- Hugging Face Daily Papers as a source (its terms are silent on automated use and its "trending" is star-driven; arXiv gives the same signal without the question), Semantic Scholar (no key), Wikipedia and Google Trends (celebrity spikes), social attention of any kind, Altmetric, press feeds.
- Click counters, a field picker, ordering chips from the person's local chats (v2, only with the owner's word and behind the existing off-by-default memory switch).
- A model that rewrites, translates or explains a chip.
- Health topics (T6), until the owner has a review process for them.
- Real-phone and screen-reader checks: not done by any study; the keyboard-on-phone behaviour of a filled chip and the on-screen keyboard against the sticky box need a real device.

## 7. What was built: the speed of a long chat (C1, C2), 3 and 4 Nov 2026

**The gate** is `apps/web/e2e/chat-perf.mjs` ("browser: chat performance" in `integration.mjs`; alone: `node e2e/chat-perf.mjs [baseUrl]` against the Worker). A stored chat of 20 replies of 20 papers (22,300 DOM nodes) is opened with the processor four times slower, the stand-in model streams one reply under it, and the browser's own instruments are read: Event Timing at 16 ms (a key pressed with nothing being written, and four during the stream), long tasks, frames, layout shift, CDP's script, layout and style time. It also scrolls up through the whole chat (nothing on screen may move but by the scroll, the page may not change height by more than 2%), checks print, the Tab key, the focus, a jump to a cited card and the ring round it, a 31-character word and a 100-character address at 320 and 360 px, and the number of live regions. **Its lines are not the plan's starting lines**: those came from a bare page of plain cards (open in 1.2 s, a key in 200 ms, a task of 150 ms), and the page's real cards (motion components, a menu each) cost a reply of five cards about 150 to 250 ms alone at this speed. The same reply is therefore first streamed under a chat of ONE turn, in the same run, and each processor-bound line is "at most so many times that, plus 100 ms" together with a wide line in milliseconds, because the same build opened the chat in 1.4 s on a quiet machine and in 7 s on a busy one. It was run five times in a row without a failure. It was shown to fail on the code as it was (six or seven lines) and to fail when scroll anchoring is switched off (72 px), when the one-value `contain-intrinsic-size` is used (the page's height changes by 43%), and when the long-word fix is taken away (11,357 elements past a 360 px window).

**Before and after** (the old and the new build served one after the other in the same window, three pairs each; 4x slower processor, 20 x 20 chat; a quiet window, then a busy one):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| The stored chat is on screen | 2.8 to 3.0 s, then 5.1 to 5.6 s | 1.4 to 1.8 s, then 2.3 to 2.9 s |
| Layout and style while it opens | 1.2 s, then 2.0 to 2.3 s | 0.20 s, then 0.27 to 0.38 s |
| A key, nothing being written | 0.71 to 0.80 s, then 1.1 to 1.7 s | 56 to 72 ms, then 80 to 104 ms |
| A key while the reply is written | 1.85 to 2.4 s, then 2.4 to 2.8 s | 152 to 232 ms, then 264 to 528 ms |
| Longest task of the stream | 0.96 to 1.2 s, then 1.3 to 1.5 s | 167 to 175 ms, then 221 to 381 ms |
| Slowest frame of the stream | 1.4 to 1.5 s, then 2.2 to 2.8 s | 174 to 191 ms, then 222 to 382 ms |
| Live regions in the 20-reply chat | 60 | 1 |
| Layout shift while 100 words arrive above 20 cards (a stream written for the purpose) | desktop 0.46, phone 1.37 | desktop 0.14, phone 0.47 |

The same reply under a chat of one turn takes a key 88 to 120 ms and has a longest task of 146 to 160 ms (quiet window): the long chat now adds about 60 to 110 ms to a key and 15 to 25 ms to the longest task; it added 2 s and 1 s.

**What each change was worth** (cumulative, measured as it went in, on a machine busier than at the end: a key with nothing being written / a key during the stream / the longest task of the stream; before all of them 1.3 to 1.6 s / 3.4 to 3.7 s / 1.8 s): memoised turns and cards with stable props 0.74 s / 1.0 s / 0.6 s; the motion providers above the app 0.58 s / 0.8 s / 0.55 s; `presenceAffectsLayout={false}` on the Home and results switch 0.16 to 0.26 s / 0.4 to 0.5 s / 0.3 s; `content-visibility` 0.12 s / 0.37 s / 0.27 s, and frames from 6 to 15 a second; the answer as one text node 0.09 s / 0.35 s / 0.26 s; cards that no longer measure their layout or count up once the reply is finished 0.10 s / 0.35 s / 0.27 s, and the opening of the chat from 6.0 s to 3.9 s (layout 1.5 s to 0.35 s, style 0.9 s to 0.2 s); one status line instead of three or more a reply, no change in speed.

**Found on the way, not in the plan:**
- Memoising the turns was not enough: `LazyMotion` gives its context a new object every time it renders, and it rendered with `App` (for every key, every word), which drew every motion component of the page again; and `AnimatePresence` hands the whole page below it a new presence context each time it renders (default `presenceAffectsLayout`). The providers now sit above `App` (`MotionRoot` in `App.tsx`, used by `main.tsx`), and the Home and results switch says `presenceAffectsLayout={false}` (one thing is shown at a time). This touches every page: the browser suites were run on it.
- `content-visibility: auto` has a size the browser remembers (`contain-intrinsic-block-size: auto`) even for a reply that is not skipped, which is why every reply carries it and only the older ones are skipped; without that, the reply above the one being asked about changed height when it was first skipped (a layout shift of 0.53). The estimate for a reply that was never drawn is the number of its papers and whether it has an answer, times the measured height of a card at four widths (`--cv-*` in `shell.css`). The plan's worry that a wrong estimate makes the page jump was an artefact of how the jump was measured: scroll anchoring moves the page by the difference, and what is on screen moves by 0 to 1 px even with an estimate 40% out. The estimate matters for the scroll bar and for a browser without scroll anchoring.
- A skipped reply is painted clipped to itself, so the 3 px ring that marks a cited card was cut off at its edges; `overflow-clip-margin: 24px` gives it back (tested by pixels). The last four items of the list are never skipped (the last two questions and their replies), because the reply above the new question was skipped in the same frame that the page went to it, which the browser counted as a shift.
- The streamed answer is one text node with room kept for the lines to come (6 lines, 11 on a phone), in the type of the final answer; the Ask panel of a paper keeps its fading words.
- The long-word fix (`minmax(0, 1fr)`, `overflow-wrap: anywhere`) was already in the style sheet from the chat release; the gate now holds it (31 characters at 320 and 360 px).
- A render-phase side effect: `takeHandoff` removed the text Home hands to Check references and Idea Check inside a `useState` initialiser, and React draws a page twice when something below it waits (seen once the page's code was already in the browser): the second drawing found nothing and the page opened with an empty box. With the new build this happened in 1 of 4 tries in the browser suite (the old build never in 40); the cause of the second drawing was not found. `readHandoff` now only reads, and the page clears it once it is on screen (`clearHandoff`). 30 of 30 tries pass.

**Things to know.** The word-by-word fade of a streamed answer in the chat is gone, and a gap shows under the "Writing" step until the lines fill it (screenshots in the report). An element inside a skipped reply has an empty `innerText` until it is drawn (`textContent` has it; one check of `chat.mjs` used it) and axe cannot see what lies behind it, so in the dark and reading themes it reports its buttons as light text on white (`chat.mjs` draws every reply for its axe runs; Lighthouse would report them too). Select-all and copy, print (45 pages with and without the rule) and the Tab key work through skipped replies; find-in-page was not tried with a real Ctrl+F (MDN says it works). The box on Home still measures its layout for every word of a reply (about 6% of the main thread in a stream): it is what makes the box glide, and was left alone. **Watch the Home layout shift** (`perf.mjs`, limit 0.1): it is the swap from the fallback font to the page's fonts, and it depends on whether the first paint comes before the fonts do. The old build gave 0.000 or 0.081; this one paints a little earlier on a quiet machine and gave 0.000, 0.085 and, in 2 of 12 runs, 0.157. Variants of this build with each change taken out in turn gave the same spread (it moves with timing, not with a change), so it is not a result of the work here; it needs its own fix with the Home work (a fallback font with matching metrics, or `font-display: optional` for the heading's font).

## 10. What was built: script, language and the look (C5, C6), 4 Nov 2026

Nothing here is committed or live. The pure rules are in `packages/shared/src/script.ts` (tested in `apps/api/src/chat/script.test.ts`, 19 tests when written (22 after the review fixes of section 12), written first and seen red; two mutations of the detector each failed the right test); the page is `ChatView.tsx`, `ResultCard.tsx`, `Shell.tsx`, `HomePage.tsx`, `shell.css` and `tokens.css`; the browser suite is `apps/web/e2e/chat-look.mjs` ("browser: chat script and look (C5, C6)" in `integration.mjs`; it was written first and seen red on the code as it was, and eight mutations of the page each failed it).

**Language and direction (C5).** `textLang(text)` says `hi` when Devanagari letters are MORE THAN HALF of the letters of the first 600 characters (letters and the marks that go with them: digits, signs and spaces are not counted; exactly half is not a majority; a Devanagari vowel sign and the virama are marks and not letters, and counting letters alone made a sentence with most of its words in Hindi and a few in English come out as English: section 12); text in Latin letters is never marked, however Hindi its words (Hinglish stays the page's own `en`). `textAttrs(text, typed?)` is what an element gets: `lang="hi" dir="auto"` for Hindi; for what a person typed also `dir="auto"` for any script (W3C: text written by people may run from the right). It is put on the question's bubble, the box and the editor (`dir`), the answer, the live words and the background text, the sentences behind an answer, the lines "Using your earlier question" and "Used from your earlier chat", a paper's title, authors, venue and abstract, the chat's title and its name in the rail. The page stays `lang="en"`. Devanagari is also written for Marathi and Nepali: they are marked `hi` too, which is better for a voice than English and wrong only in the name. ### 13.7 Why the launch is `off` and not `dark` (decided on 4 Nov 2026, evening, from the verification run)

The first design committed `"TRENDING": "dark"`: the job runs every night, the route hides the list, the owner reads it. The verification run then made the first run of the job's logic on real OpenAlex and arXiv data, and it was a partial one (45 of 243 subfields, no key, 17 fields): it published no file, because only 2 of the 40 pool phrases passed everything and 8 are needed (19 fell at arXiv's second-source check, 16 at the score, 1 at the verifier; the India pool of 14 had none). A partial scan is no proof of what the whole scan gives, but it is a real signal that the yield may be under 8. Three consequences of `dark` follow: (1) a job that fails every night makes `/api/v1/health/jobs` answer 503, and the hourly Watchdog opens a GitHub issue and sends an e-mail for it, in the same check as the clean-up; (2) the job runs in the same invocation as the clean-up and the retraction re-check, with the same 6 connections and the same subrequest limit, and no real run has measured it; (3) it spends the OpenAlex key's day, which the product's own search shares, at 08:47 in India. None of these touches a visitor's data, but all three would be paid for before a single real list had been looked at. So the committed state is `off`: the job is stopped (a night is reported as `skipped: off`, a good run, so health stays 200), the route hides the list, and the first real list is made by hand (runbook, LAUNCH DARK, step 2). The way on is one push each: `dark`, then the line out. This is a change of the order of the owner's own rule (nobody sees a list the owner has not read), made in the same direction (more hidden, not less), and it is the owner's to reverse. For the same reason the privacy page's Trending bullet is held back from this deploy (section 13.6); it goes out with the push that sets `dark`.
