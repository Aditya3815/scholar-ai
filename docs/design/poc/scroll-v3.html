    Chapter VI · Your own papers

    pilot-study.pdf · in My Workspace

    Sample size of a pilot study for a two-arm trial

    Pilot studies are often used to estimate the variance needed to plan a main trial.

    Small pilots give unstable estimates, and a main study planned from them is often under-powered.

    We compare rules for the pilot's size by the chance that the main study ends up with less power than planned.

    Numerical examples use a power of 80%why 80%? and a standardised effect of 0.4.

    The rules are compared over pilot sizes from 10 to 70 per group, and for effects between 0.2 and 0.8.

    Section 2 sets out the model; section 3 the rules compared; section 4 the results.

    p. 1 / 15

    4. Results

    Table 2 gives the pilot sizes that keep the risk of an under-powered main study at 30%, 20% and 10%.

    The main study requires NL = 158 subjects per group.

    With a pilot of 12 per group, the chance of an under-powered main study is close to one in three.

    Larger pilots lower that risk quickly at first and slowly after about 35 per group.

    The same pattern holds for the smaller and the larger effects examined.
