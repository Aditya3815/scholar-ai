Every claim points at a numbered paper. The number is a promise, and the next steps check it.

2 · Follow it to the sentence

It lands on one sentence.

Not a paper, not a page: the sentence of the abstract that says it, word for word, found by the program and not written by the model.

3 · Is the record real?

The DOI has to resolve.

The record is looked up at the registry and its title has to agree. A DOI that does not resolve is removed, never shown.

Retractions are called out first.

A paper that was withdrawn is marked at the top and is never used as evidence.

Questions, answered plainly.

Does it ever write a citation itself?

No. A model names a paper and a sentence number; the program turns that pair into the abstract's own sentence. A mark that does not point at a checked sentence is removed.

Will it tell me whether my idea is new?

No. It shows the closest papers and the gaps. Not finding a paper means it is not in these databases, not that it does not exist.

What does a literature scan keep, and for how long?

The topic you wrote, the plan you approved and the checked report, for 24 hours. Only the English search words you approved are sent to public indexes. You can delete a scan at any time.

How much of my day does a scan use?

About 8,000 to 15,000 AI tokens of the 60,000 you have a day, and the page tells you before it starts.

Which sources does it search?

OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, PubMed, arXiv and CORE, plus Europe PMC for full text of open-access papers.
