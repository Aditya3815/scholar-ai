# Understanding the message, and the state of one chat (research and plan, 3 Nov 2026)

Asked by the owner on 3 Nov 2026, after he typed "Can you search about convoluton nueral netwrk all papers" (Auto) and got about 17 records that had nothing to do with it. This note says what happens today and why, what is decided, what is built in which order, and what is still the owner's to decide. It follows [chat-actions-context.md](chat-actions-context.md) (which made the chat remember inside a conversation) and [chatview-and-trending.md](chatview-and-trending.md) (which is being built now). Method: [how-we-work.md](../how-we-work.md).

**Status: a plan. Nothing in it is built.** The build starts after the ChatView, bundle, Trending and Home work (workflow `wf_f1cfc40d-0d6`) is verified and pushed (section 9 says which files collide and in what order they merge).

**How it was made.** Five read-only studies and a probe campaign (about 235 to 246 production probes, the studies' own two counts, as a visitor, over 185 labelled messages in 28 categories, plus code traces) found the cause; a second set of six studies and a critic looked at conversation context and state. Three designers then wrote three complete designs from three angles (smallest risk and staged; one unified pipeline; evidence and instruments first) and three judges scored them on the product's guarantees and the owner's rules, on engineering risk, and on what a person actually sees. This note is the one final plan: the "staged" design is the base and the judges' named grafts and fixes are in it. The study reports are in the session record, not in the repository (as chat-actions-context.md says of its own); every number below names its source in square brackets, for example [r3-trace], and says how far to trust it. **A red team then attacked this note; its blocker, high and medium findings were each checked against the code and are applied (Appendix B says what was done for each, and where a finding was adapted and not applied as written).**

**How to read it.** Section 4 is the plan. Sections 5 to 7 hold the exact sentences the page will speak and the state. Section 9 is the schedule, 10 the privacy text per release, 11 what is not done, 12 what only the owner can decide.

## 1. What was measured (the numbers, with their honesty notes)

In 94 of 94 "understood" events of the category probes the search words were the message as typed; the other 2 of the 96 cases got HTTP 400 [r3-categories].
- OpenAlex, PubMed and DOAJ need every word to match. For the typed message they returned **0 hits**; Semantic Scholar was measured once, keyless, by one study (a zero), and the providers study got HTTP 429 on 15 of 15 calls, so its answer to the typed message is one zero and not a measurement; Crossref is fuzzy, never answers empty, and **answered alone**: 2,806,409 matches, 17 records shown, 1 with an abstract. Twins collapsed in 6 of 12 Crossref-only lists and a promoted twin was number 1 in 4 of 12 (clean queries included) [r3-trace]. The effect on five-provider lists is unmeasured [r3-critic].
- Nothing in the chat path checks that a result is about the topic. A model-free gate exists (`apps/api/src/litscan/relevance.ts`, `isRelevant`, :33) and the literature scan uses it; the chat does not. On the owner's 17 records it passes 0 of 17 [r3-trace].
- Provider strings are shown raw: `crossref.ts:42` (title) and :54 (venue) copy fields as sent. 19% of 200 Crossref venues carried an HTML entity in one sample and 6.7% of 476 in another (the samples disagree; both are real), 5.5% of titles carried an entity, 1% of titles carried a newline or tabs; 27% of Crossref records have no author [r3-trace], [r3-providers].

The frame cleaner alone does **not** fix the owner's example: with only "Can you search about ... all papers" removed, the list is still records that carry the same typos mixed with unrelated PubMed records (a production probe of the staged designer, 3 Nov, 16.6 s cold); the corrected phrase "convolution neural network" gave 13 of 13 on-topic records with four sources contributing [r5-staged 0.2]. **Limits and costs that shape the plan:** a person's day is 60,000 free Workers AI tokens (`apps/api/src/llm/config.ts`; the allowance, D6, is undecided); a visitor has no model at all; a Cloudflare Worker on the paid plan (10,000 subrequests, 30 s CPU by default; from docs/roadmap.md, not seen on the dashboard); provider lanes (Crossref one start per 340 ms, three at once; Semantic Scholar one a second with a 3 s queue limit; OpenAlex about $0.001 an uncached call) and the 27 Oct 429 storm (`apps/api/src/gate/index.ts`, the literature-scan comment) are what limits fan-out; production time to the last event is p50 5.3 to 5.6 s, p90 8.4 s (the 2 to 3 s figure was a cached repeat) [r3-critic].

**How far to trust all of this.** No model was run by anyone: not the router on a messy message, not a plan call, not the writer, not the judge; every probe was a visitor, so what a signed-in person gets for the owner's message was never seen. All 185 cases were written by the researchers after the owner's example was known, one rater, and several numbers (the 98% of the architecture ladder, the validator's 40 of 40, the term-coverage AUC of 0.94, the resolver's 85% and 99%) were tuned on the cases they score. About 235 to 246 of the probes (the studies' two counts) came from one IP in 20 minutes. Nothing counts how often real messages are messy, so every "how often" is a count of labelled cases, not a share of traffic. The two helpline numbers (112 and Tele-MANAS 14416) and every Hinglish and Hindi sentence below are unverified and not read by a native speaker. **Do not quote any number in this note as a measured result of the finished product;** R3 exists so that real shares can be read, and the blind hold-out of E0 is the first honest number.

## 3. The pipeline: understanding, retrieval, presentation, state

### 3.1 One flow, from (state, message) to the new state

Only the hooks are new. Each is one call into a small pure module, placed in `apps/api/src/chat/routes.ts` inside the one handler (`chatRoutes.post("/")`, working tree :173 to :392; function names, not line numbers, are the stable anchors while `wf_f1cfc40d` is still moving lines).

| Step | What | Code or model | Release |
|---|---|---|---|

**Why this order.** The lane runs before the router because a person describing chest pain must not wait for a router call, and the router prompt itself calls small talk and orders "search" (`router.ts:41`): a model cannot be the safety net. It runs before small talk because small talk already answers "help me" with a greeting. Tool cues run after the route because they may only turn a rules or fallback search into an offer ("A tool chosen by hand is never overridden", router-w3.md). The reading runs after the cues because it only matters for a message that will be searched. Tiers run after retrieval and **inside** `shape`, so the interim snapshots, the card numbers and the writer's [n] marks all come from one order; folding after numbering would break the marks when "Check strictly" re-reads them [r3-critic trap 4].

**Amended 4 Nov 2026 (the owner's review, 12b.1).** Two things in the order above were wrong. (1) The lanes of U3 read a message alone, but a follow-up cannot be read alone: `topicWordCount("papers from 2018 to 2021")` is 0 (the docstring of `subjectWords`), so "only after 2020" would have been answered by the no-topic question before the router saw it, and "translate that into Hindi" can be taken for a request the box cannot do, when the follow-up path already serves it (`follow?.answerLanguage`, `routes.ts:326`; R21 class R). Small talk is already safe (`isSmallTalk(text, rctx)` takes the context). So a pure state READ (U1b) now runs right after the safety lane, and U3 stands back for a follow-up. (2) An id is found by a pattern, not by judgement, yet R13 acted only over a rules or fallback result: when the rules abstain (an "I", a tool word) the model is called and "never over the model's choice" would then skip the lookup after a wasted call. Ids, a reference list and a long paste are now settled by code before the router (U1c). Safety stays first, and every lane still only ever stands back: what U1b does not catch falls to U4 exactly as before.

### 3.2 The Reading: one object, used as the plan and as the plan echo

`packages/shared/src/reading.ts` (new, types only, about 40 lines):

```
Reading = {
  v: 1,
  source: "rules" | "model" | "resolver",
  topic: string,                      the words searched, at most 120 characters
  left: string[],                     phrases taken out: each an EXACT substring of the message, at most 5, each at most 40 characters
  notes: ("all" | "recent" | "count" | "negation" | "form" | "order" | "author" | "venue" | "threshold" | "prove" | "scrub" | "person")[],
  assumed: { from: string, to: string, why: "spelling" | "acronym" }[],   at most 3
  suggested: { from: string, to: string }[],      spelling offers that were NOT applied
  years?: { from?: number, to?: number }, count?: number (1 to 50),
  answerLanguage?: "en" | "hi" | "hinglish",
  ids?: { kind: "doi" | "arxiv" | "pmid", value: string }[],
  lane?: "care" | "decision",
  ask?: { kind: "acronym" | "broad", term: string, options: { label: string, message: string }[] }
}
```

`checkReading(reading, text)` (new, in `apps/api/src/understand/reading-check.ts`) runs on the server before anything is sent and is pure: every `left[]` and `assumed[].from` is a substring of the message; every year is 1900 to 2100 and appears in the message as a number; `count` appears in the message; every `options[].message` is built by code from the message and a closed table; at most 600 characters in all. A reading that fails the check is dropped (the old line "I understood this as ..." and the whole message as before). It rides on two **optional** fields of events that exist (`understood.reading`, `AssistantMsg.reading`): the reducer ignores a field it does not know (`applyChatEvent`, `packages/shared/src/chat-state.ts:83`), and a notice of an unknown kind is shown as text (the reducer takes any kind, :95-96). No new event type exists anywhere in this plan; an old open tab shows the old line and, for a new reason, an empty reason line.

The words the page says about a reading are **not reworded**: "I understood this as", "I searched for" and "Use my exact words" are pinned in `apps/web/e2e/chat.mjs` (about lines 1903 to 2052), `apps/web/src/workspace/intro/Film.tsx:508`, `apps/api/src/legal-text.test.ts:54` and `apps/api/scripts/chat-e2e.mjs`. The first release that changes what a person sees sets `understood.searchQuery` whenever the words changed, which makes the existing "I searched for ... Use my exact words" line appear with no new web code; new lines are added under it.

### 3.4 What each provider is sent

Today: one string, the whole message, to all five providers (`SCOPES.all` in `apps/api/src/search/index.ts:18-21`): OpenAlex `title_and_abstract.search` (`openalex.ts:60`, every word must match), Semantic Scholar, Crossref `query.bibliographic` (fuzzy), PubMed, DOAJ (at most 8 words joined by AND). The cache key is (provider, normalised query, 20) only (`index.ts:46`).

After R8 to R14: the same one string for all five, but it is the **read topic** (frame removed, then possibly the confirmed spelling), not the message, so messy variants of one topic share one cache entry (more hits, fewer paid OpenAlex calls). Hand-chosen: the words as written. **Not changed:** per-provider syntax, any year, type, author or institution filter at a provider, OpenAlex `search.semantic`, a Crossref filter, `meta.count` (the provider interface returns a bare list). Each of those needs the cache key to carry the filter and an OpenAlex call-shape experiment (OpenAlex answered 429 to every study after about 25 keyless calls), and none is needed to fix the family of failures this plan targets. Years and count are applied after retrieval on the papers' own fields, as follow-ups already do (`YEAR_FILTER_SEARCH_LIMIT = 40`, `packages/shared/src/chat-context.ts`).

Calls per message: 6 to 9 outbound fetches measured (6 to 7 without Semantic Scholar, 8 to 9 with it), about 40 KV writes; the trace study's estimate for production is 8 to 20 outbound calls on a cold search [r3-trace]. A repair search (R14) doubles that for the few messages that carry a spelling candidate and whose typed words show no strong paper (expected 2 to 5% of English one-liners: unknown until R3 counts it). It is sequential, never parallel with the first, so the 27 Oct storm pattern is not repeated.

### 3.5 The budget, and what degrades first

Signed-in tokens against the 60,000 a day (D6 is still the owner's). Measured: first answer 9,258 tokens, follow-up answered 10,565 (router 1,403 in and 681 out, writer 3,110 in and 862 out, judge 3,040 in and 125 out) [r4-audit ledger, 3 Nov]. Estimated (unmeasured): plan call 1,500 to 1,700 tokens.

| Path | Model tokens | Seconds added | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitor, any message | 0 | 0 (code time **unmeasured**; R12's own gate is p95 under 15 ms a message) | reading, tiers, lanes, lookups |
| Lane reply, ask card, id lookup, no-topic question | 0 | 0 to 1.5 (lookup) | no search or no answer |
| First message, strong papers, answer | about 9,258 (as today) | 0 | |
| First message, no strong paper | about 0 (writer not called) | 0 | saves about 9,000 on a bad search |
| First message, flagged, model plan (R23) | about 9,258 + 1,700 | +0.8 typical, 3 s cap | rambling, misspelt, nothing close |

At 60,000 a day that is about 6.5 first answers, 5.5 flagged ones, 6.0 model follow-ups. `FOLLOW_UP_TOKENS_ESTIMATE` (`packages/shared/src/chat-context.ts`, 11,000, one static number) becomes a table keyed by path, **fitted from the production ledger after R3 and two weeks of real rows** (R21); until then it is left alone.

**Degrade order when something runs short** (each step independent, each with a switch; the page says in the reading line when a step was skipped): 1. the plan call is skipped (visitor; allowance too low; a per-isolate circuit breaker after 3 failures in 60 seconds, because the Workers AI limits page states 300 requests a minute, or 20 a minute per model for models that need the paid plan, and does not name gpt-oss-120b); 2. the repair search is skipped (a provider partial, a lane busy); 3. the follow-up model call is skipped (the resolver, else "current topic plus message" papers with no answer); 4. the written answer is skipped (allowance below about 9,500, or no strong paper); 5. the judge, `CHAT_JUDGE=off`, as today. **Never dropped:** the lanes (safety first), `plainText` at the adapter, the truth of the reading line, and "history and state never reach the judge".

### 3.6 What happens when a step fails (never a confident wrong list)

- R1 to R22 use no model; nothing can time out except the providers, which already degrade to the "partial" notice.
- The tiers (R9) guard against a confident wrong list from any cause: a list with no strong paper never reaches the writer, and says so.
- The follow-up router (`routeFollowUp`, `apps/api/src/chat/router.ts:408`): after a failed attempt (a malformed reply is tried once more, a timeout is not) it returns the raw words as a "fallback" search with `fellBack` true, and `routes.ts` then writes and judges an answer about whatever those words found (9 of 188 calls failed; between 4 and 9 follow-ups fell back, about 2 to 5%: section 1). R5 changes that policy and counts the real share.
- The plan call (R23): a 3-second soft budget; on timeout, error, invalid JSON or a reading that fails `checkReading`, the pipeline uses the model-free reading of U6 exactly as a visitor gets it and counts `plan_fallback`. It never falls back to the raw message and retries once only for a malformed reply (`router.ts:96-106` does the same today).
- A model-free reading that is "unread" (more than 6 content words: a ramble): the message is searched as written, exactly as today, with no tiers and no claim; this stays unfixed until R23 (the rambling class scored 0.13 at precision 10). A message with no subject word at all ("42", "ok then") gets a question, not a search (R10), unless it is a call for help, which the safety lane answers first (R4).

## 4. The releases, in order

### 4.0 How every release ships

- **Before any code:** this note, updated by each release (how-we-work section 1). **Tests first:** written, run, seen to fail for the stated reason.
- **The flag.** Three positions: `off` (today's behaviour, byte for byte), `shadow` (compute and count with R3's counters, change nothing on the wire: no model call, no provider call, no network), `on`. Gate flags are unset-is-off (P3); the release commit sets `shadow` in `apps/api/wrangler.jsonc` (its own comment at :50 says a switch that must survive a deploy goes in the `vars` block; a hand-set dashboard variable is replaced by the next CI deploy). The local integration stack copies that file, so a flag that is `on` there is `on` in the browser suites too. **No trailing `//` comment on a `vars` line:** `apps/api/scripts/integration.mjs:24-25` reads `wrangler.jsonc` with a filter that drops only whole-line comments, so a trailing comment would break every local suite (a unit test reads the file the same way and fails first). A flag can also be **lowered, never raised,** by a KV override (H0), which is how a tripwire acts and is a faster rollback than a deploy.
- **Flagless releases and rollback time.** R6a, R6b, R7, R18's browser fixes, R19 and R22 have no switch: a rollback is `git revert`, the CI run and the deploy, **about 15 to 20 minutes** (the CI run is most of it). R18(a), the one of them that can stop a message from being sent, gets a server-delivered switch (4.18).
- **Every release has a gate.** A numeric gate in 8.5, or the words "none, because ..." there. A rate is read on the dev set with its interval and the sealed hold-out is read once, at the end (8.5).
- **Shadow is only trusted with a number.** A shadow period ends when the offline eval's prediction for that counter and the production count agree within a factor of two **and** there are at least 300 events for the counter, or the owner signs off in writing on about 40 reading lines read by hand (R3 is the first release that can say how many messages there are at all). If production is three times the prediction, something is wrong and nothing has hurt anyone.
- **The invariant (E5).** With every gate flag at `off` and at `shadow`, the stream of every request in the differential list is byte-identical (section 8.4). A release that is meant to change a first message re-records its own golden **by hand, on purpose**, in its own commit.
- **Verification before each push** (how-we-work section 4): `npm run typecheck`; `npx vitest run` in `apps/api`; `npm run eval` (G1 to G6 and the prior-art eval; before and after numbers in the PR); `npm run build` then `npm run budget -w apps/web` for any web change (**both** budgets: the first-load script, 250 KB, and the first-load style sheet, 26 KB, of which 24.7 KB is used, so 1.3 KB is left: `chat-actions-context.md:182`. New chat styles go in a stylesheet imported by the lazy `ChatView` chunk so that they are split out of the first-load sheet, and a release that cannot do that says so in its PR); `node scripts/integration.mjs --browser` in `apps/api` for anything the browser sees; push; CI green; `/api/v1/health` on the live address; `/admin` counters after the shadow period.
- **The D2 review pack, for every release that changes a sentence of the privacy page:** (a) `npx vitest run src/legal-text.test.ts src/legal-match.test.ts` with the new pins written first; (b) the Idea Check checks and the two browser suites the text touches: `node scripts/integration.mjs --browser --only="idea check,chat integration,browser: legal pages,browser: chat"` (a substring match on the suite labels in `integration.mjs`: "idea check integration", "chat integration", "browser: idea check", "browser: idea check in the conversation", "browser: legal pages", "browser: chat (prompt box)" and "browser: chat performance"), `npx vitest run src/idea src/chat/context-before.test.ts src/chat/history.test.ts`, and `npm run eval` (prior-art); (c) an independent reader who has not seen the code reads the diff of `apps/web/src/components/legal/LegalPages.tsx` against the call sites and writes, in the PR, for each changed sentence the `file:line` that makes it true (a sentence with no code path is deleted); (d) the text is its own commit, after the code; (e) `PRIVACY_VERSION` changes only where a release says "plus bump", with the old version added to `RETIRED_VERSIONS` (`apps/api/src/legal-text.test.ts:26`) in the same commit; (f) the owner reads each changed sentence as it was built, before its go-live (the up-front read of the queue is of a draft, 10.1).

### 4.5 R4: the safety lane

*Amended 4 Nov 2026 (the owner's review): a muted topic note under the papers, see 12b.2; the false-positive negatives already include self-harm research topics.*

The highest-harm family, small and model-free, in the first behavioural wave and independent of the cleaner. A pure module `apps/api/src/understand/lane.ts` (`safetyRead(text)`) and one hook in `routes.ts` **before** small talk (the `talk` line, `isSmallTalk(text, rctx)`, working tree :277) and before routing. **What the code does today with these messages** (the repository's own `route.ts`, bundled and run offline on 3 Nov: the visitor path, and the rules half of a signed-in one). "I am suicidal", "I keep thinking about suicide", "I have suicidal thoughts", "I need help" and "please help me I cant do this anymore" are all searched (`offlineRoute`: search, how fallback, confidence 0.5), so the person gets papers. "I want to kill myself" gives mode idea by rules, because `IDEA_CUE` contains "i want to" (`route.ts:80`), so a visitor is offered Idea Check. "help" and "help me" are small talk (`route.ts:154`, `help(?: me)?`) and get the greeting that says what the box does.

**How it reads.** The **whole** Auto message is read, which can be up to 20,000 characters (`routes.ts:213`): distress is often written long, so no message is skipped for its length. The text is lower-cased and folded (NFKC, curly apostrophes, contractions such as "cant"), cut into words once, and every run of 1 to 6 words is looked up in closed sets (a JavaScript `Set` of phrases; no regular expression with a repeat over the text, so nothing can backtrack and the time grows in a straight line with the length). A phrase is a contiguous run of words, which is also what makes the research exclusion work: "I am doing research on suicide" contains no phrase of any set, and "I am suicidal" contains one. Hinglish and Devanagari Hindi phrases are word runs in the same sets; where a verb has a gendered form both forms are listed, and a test fails when one has no twin. The lists are written in E0 from the dev set (8.2), not the other way round.

**Order, and what is not covered.** The strongest tier that fires decides: CRISIS, then URGENT, then HELP, then DECISION, then CARE. CRISIS, URGENT and HELP replace the papers. If CRISIS and URGENT both fire (an overdose and "I want to die") the reply is S-CRISIS, which carries the emergency line. **Named, not hidden:** someone being hurt (abuse, violence), a child in danger and any other emergency that is not a symptom of the sender or of a person the sender describes are **not** in this lane's first wave; those messages are searched like any other. O-16 asks the owner whether they join `core` once their sentences and numbers have been checked as carefully as these.

**Framing never exempts.** A sentence that frames the person as a researcher, student or clinician does not stop an intent phrase or an emergency-now from firing: "I am studying for exams and I want to kill myself" and "my patient is having a stroke now" are in the positives of both sets. (The evidence design let framing exempt a message from URGENT; that is a false negative on exactly the messages that matter, and a false block costs one press.) The cost of a false CARE or DECISION line is one gentle sentence. What a research frame does do is stop the bare topic words from counting: "suicide" and "suicidal" are not triggers at all, so there is nothing for a frame to exempt.

### 4.6 R5: the follow-up failure policy

**Server only, own flag, the first state-track fix.** Today, when the follow-up router fails (a malformed reply twice, a timeout once: `routeFollowUp`, `router.ts:408-430`, returns `fellBack` with the check `model_failed`), the page searches the raw words and **writes and judges an answer about whatever they found**: in the ledger of 3 Nov 9 of 188 router calls failed, so between 4 and 9 follow-ups fell back, about 2 to 5% [r4-audit]. ## 6. Ambiguity (D3) made concrete

### 6.1 The rule, in words

D3 asks for the least friction with the most accuracy. Here that is **a decision table that runs in code, from signals that are counted, with closed tables the owner can read and edit.** A model is not the judge of when to ask (CLAMBER found models poor at judging their own ambiguity and Zou et al. found low and middle quality questions drive people away; and models under-ask once they hold retrieved text [r3-leaders], [r3-format-ux]), so the decision comes from code **before** the search and **before** any paid step. A model may add a flag after R23, never the options. The evidence that one good question helps is from other domains (an oracle setting: Aliannejadi 2019, over 170% on precision at 1); no A/B for a paper-search box exists.

### 6.2 The signals

S1 the subject-word count after the frame is removed (`subjectWords(topic).length`, `route.ts:375`: 0 means incomplete, 1 may mean broad); S2 a closed acronym table; S3 a closed broad-field table; S4 a spelling candidate and whether a second search confirms it (R12, R14); S5 the tier counts after retrieval (R9); S6 the provider signature (only one provider answered while the all-words providers returned nothing: `one_source`, R3); S7 (later) index counts: OpenAlex returns `meta.count` in the same response (`openalex.ts:66-69` reads `results` and drops it), but reading it is a provider-interface change and is not in R1 to R23; S8 (later) the plan call's ambiguity enum.

### 6.4 The tables (data in the code, edited by the owner, one reviewed pull request per change)

**`acronyms.ts`:** `{ acronym, match: "caps" | "any", mode: "ask" | "assume", force?: "owner", senses: [{ label, expansion, cues[], share }], note? }`. "caps" means the acronym matches only in capitals (so "ad", "us", "it", "as" in a sentence never trigger); "any" (ms, ml, cnn, tb) matches lower case too, but only when the acronym is one of at most two subject words.
**The rule that sets the mode is measured, not felt.** A build script (`apps/api/scripts/measure-senses.mjs`, new; about 120 OpenAlex count calls, under 0.15 USD, run by a person and reviewed row by row, re-measured every quarter; public data only; no runtime call, no model) records for each sense the count of works that match the term together with the sense words, and the share of the term's works. **Ask** when the leading sense's share is below 0.70 **and** at least two senses have a share of 0.15 or more **and** each of those counts at least 1,000 works; otherwise **assume**. The shares in the starter table are **not yet measured** except where this note says so.
**CNN is the owner's.** D3 names it as an ask; the data points the other way without measuring it: in one probe 11 of 13 shown records are the convolutional-network sense and one is "The CNN paradigm" (1993, cellular neural networks); in another study 70 of 70 records for 7 query variants contain CNN, convolution or neural somewhere (a count that is circular for the word CNN: one record is "CNN code of conduct", and it is not a share of senses, which stays unmeasured until `measure-senses.mjs` runs); production already behaves as "assume" silently. The entry is marked `force: "owner"` and ships as an **ask**; only the owner's written word turns it into an assumption (O-4). With no press counters (O-5) there is nothing that flips it by itself, which is the safe direction.

### 6.6 How a paid step is gated

Three paid things exist: the written answer (about 9,000 of the 60,000 daily tokens), Idea Check (a model plan and a search) and a Literature scan (about 6,600 tokens a theme, 26,400 for four). The last two already start only on a click with an estimate shown; chat starts neither. For the written answer this plan gates in four places: (1) rows 1 and 2 stop before any search and any token (an ask costs nothing, and a pending ask spends nothing if ignored); (2) R9 never calls the writer for a list with no strong paper; (3) a follow-up the router could not read gets no written answer (R5); (4) a personal medicine decision gets none (R4).

### 6.7 The false-ask budget and its gates (written before the first run)

- **Ask rate:** asks (rows 1 and 2) at most 3% of the 400 research negatives (12; interval printed), the research-labelled hold-out (20 English messages) read once as an upper bound only, and at most 4% of searches in production (server counters `ask_shown` over all messages), within a factor of two of the dev share before the card is turned on.
- **Ask recall:** at least 70% of at least 30 labelled ambiguous messages (bare MS, NER, PCA, AD, ER, CNN; "MS biomarkers", "NER in clinical notes", "ML for protein folding" where the table says assume).
- **No ask where a cue exists:** 0 asks on the 40 cue-bearing messages.
- **Introduction:** fires on at least 80% of 20 broad one-word fields and at most 1% of the 400 research messages.
- **Statistics:** 3 errors in 100 can only show "no worse than about 8%"; every such result is printed with its bound (section 8.5).
- **What the adaptive part is, with no press counters:** the table's modes come from measured sense shares (re-measured quarterly), the cues come from review, and a breaker takes the card off when asks exceed 5% of a day's searches. A flip from ask to assume for an entry the owner did not force is a reviewed edit of the table. If the owner later allows press counters (O-5), the rule of the staged design applies on top: an entry whose first option is picked in at least 9 of 10 of at least 100 asks is reviewed for a flip; more than 20% "something else" means a sense is missing. **Said plainly:** with no press counters (and none for a `via` field either), D3's "adaptive" is limited to a quarterly review of the tables against measured shares and to the breaker; whether R8 cleaned wrongly (a person pressing "Use my exact words"), whether the ask card is skipped and whether a spelling chip is used are **invisible in production**, and the only proxy is the `anyway` count. O-5 offers the owner a smaller signal, one closed `via` field counted by name with no text, that would make them visible.

## 7. Conversation state inside one chat (D1: a session is one chat)

### 7.1 What D1 fixes, and what it leaves open

Session means one chat. Context between the messages of a chat is live today (chat-actions-context.md section 11): the browser sends the last 3 finished searches (`MAX_HISTORY_ENTRIES = 6` entries, `packages/shared/src/chat-context.ts:11`), a model reads them only for a message that cannot stand alone, and a visitor sends and gets nothing. It does **not** carry into a new chat on its own (only the optional Settings switch can, when the person turned it on: O-1); the Continue chip goes (R6a). The code never defined "session": it has four scopes (the chat's message list, the tab's open chat in `sessionStorage`, the sign-in cookie with a fixed 30-day `maxAge` set once at sign-in in `apps/api/src/auth/routes.ts`, and the device list and Settings switch), and the sign-in is not usable as a memory scope. From now on the words are: a **conversation** is one chat in this browser; "memory of earlier chats" is the optional cross-chat switch; "session" is used for nothing but the sign-in. The Settings switch "Use my earlier chats" is **not mentioned by the owner**: it stays exactly as it is and is open question O-1 (this plan neither widens nor removes it).

### 7.2 What is wrong today (measured)

- A stopped, errored or interrupted reply leaves no topic: `contextBefore` keeps only finished searches (`chat-state.ts:398-399`), pinned as intended by `context-before.test.ts:146`; the next follow-up silently attaches to an **older** topic.
- The history knows only the **cited** papers, with no ids; "paper 2" after a re-run is renumbered by position (same-papers re-searches the anchor words and the list can change; `Paper.id` is stable but never sent).
- A requested answer language lasts one turn; a year range is dropped by the next refinement (`router.ts:336-348`, :364-367).
- A wrong referent after a topic switch passes every server check; "go back to the first topic" is settled by the rules as a plain search and no model sees it.
- The router failure policy (R5) and the browser store (R18).
- Cost: a model-read follow-up is about 2,160 tokens in the router alone on average (input and output, p95 2,822); of the router's input (1,611 tokens on average with the real prompt builders) 83% is the fixed instruction block (`ROUTER_CONTEXT_SYSTEM`, 5,544 characters, about 1,345 tokens) and the history block is about 145 tokens; writer plus judge are about two thirds of an 11,000-token follow-up and never see history. So **a shorter history saves almost nothing; a code decision that skips the router call saves the whole call.**

### 7.3 The one design: the Reading, the plan echo, and a pure fold

There is one object, the Reading (section 3.2): the output of understanding a first message (rules now, a model after R23), the plan echo stored on each reply, the thing the fold reads, and the output of the follow-up resolver. No second record, no second schema (the critic found the state object and the query plan were two designs for one job).

`ConversationState` (`packages/shared/src/chat-fold.ts`, `foldState(path)`, pure, recomputed from `activePath(msgs)` (`chat-state.ts:286`) at **send time** and for finished replies only, never per streamed frame):

```
{
  v: 1,
  turns: number,                       finished searches on the active path, at most 99
  current: null | {
    asked: string,                     the person's own words of the last finished search, at most 200 characters
    topic: string,                     the words that search used (Reading.topic), at most 120
    lang: "en" | "hi" | "hinglish",    from the rules on that message
    years: null | { from?, to? },      the range that reply was limited to
    scope: "all" | "india",
    listIds: string[],                 state codes of the list shown, in order, at most 10 (below): never Paper.id, never a title
    answered: boolean
  },
  before: { asked: string, topic: string }[],   the two finished searches before it, oldest first (the same 3 turns the history has today)
  prefs: { answerLanguage?: "en" | "hi" | "hinglish" },   only when the person asked in words; OFF until the owner says yes (O-13)
  newTopic: boolean,                   the last user message was sent after "New topic" (R22)
  unfinished: 0 | 1 | 2,               stopped, failed or interrupted replies since the last finished search: for the honest line only, never their content
  lastAnswer?: string                  the start of the latest checked answer, at most 500 characters, marks, links and DOIs removed: exactly what the history carries today, for the writer only and only for same-papers
}
```

- **Size:** a wire budget of 2,200 characters enforced by a fixed trimming order (`listIds` to 10, then to 0; then `before`; then `asked` to 120; `current.topic` never); typical 425 characters with the first design's ids (a little less with the short state codes below); the first-draft worst case was 3,827. The cap is checked in tokens too: Devanagari is 3.5 to 3.9 characters per token against about 4.1 for English.
- **State codes, not `Paper.id`.** `Paper.id` is `doi:...`, `pmid:...` or, for a record with neither, `t:${normalizeTitle(title)}|${year}` (`merge.ts:7-9`, :30), so it can carry a stranger's title text, the very injection surface P10 keeps out. The state therefore holds `stateCode(paper)`: `p:<digits>` for a PubMed number and, for everything else, `d:` plus 8 hex characters of a 32-bit hash of `Paper.id` (a small pure function in `packages/shared`, a few lines, run identically by the browser and the server). A hash of a title is not a title, a hostile string cannot travel in it, and a code is about 10 characters, so the wire budget above is smaller still. The server never trusts a code: it recomputes the codes of its own fresh results and matches. Test: a title-only paper and a hostile title in the list; the serialised state holds no substring of any title.
- **Who updates it: nobody.** It is a function. An edit, a version switch, a retry, a reload and a second tab come out right by construction because the fold reads the active path (the "message at `upTo`" rule of `contextBefore`, :452); the replaced text of an edited message is not in the path, so it cannot leak. The existing reducers kept 10 invariants over 80,000 random steps with 0 violations [r4-evaluation].
- **The plan echo:** `AssistantMsg.reading` (optional) is set by `applyChatEvent` from `understood.reading`. An old chat, which has none, folds through a fallback to `searchQuery`, `query`, `context` and `papers` (what `historyTurn` reads today). No new store, no IndexedDB version bump.
- **What never goes into the state:** any answer text except the 500-character start above; any Idea Check or pasted text (a user message over 300 characters never counts: `askable`); **any model-written summary, standalone question or ledger** (a model-written rolling summary would put untrusted abstract text into trusted memory and cost a call; a model-written question carried forward as if it were the person's words compounds a bad restatement [r4-critic trap 9]); titles and `Paper.id` (written by strangers, or built from a title: an injection surface); anything for the judge or Check strictly.

### 7.4 The code-first resolver (R20 shadow, R21 on, class by class)

`resolveFollowUp(state, text, language)` runs on the server before the follow-up router, for a message that `needsContext` (`route.ts:417`, unchanged). It returns a Reading with an intent, or null. It decides:

| Class | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Y | a year expression and no new subject word ("only after 2020", "since 2018", "2019 ke baad", "between 2018 and 2020", "before 2015"); "recent" and "latest" name no year | refine of `current.topic` with the range |
| R | a restyle or a language request and no new subject word ("simpler", "shorter", "bullet points", "in Hindi") | same-papers; the answer language is set when asked |
| P | a paper pointer ("paper 2", "the first one", "these papers") resolved against `current.listIds` in code (an ordinal beyond the list abstains) | the server selects among its **own** fresh results by state code and keeps the original numbering; it never fetches anything the client sent |
| S | a topic switch: at least two subject words, none sharing a 5-letter stem with `current.topic` or `before[]`, no dependency cue | intent new, searched on its own words by the first-turn path (no word of the old topic comes along) |
| F | the message is only facet words from a closed lexicon (a group, a place, a design, an outcome: children, women, elderly, rural, India, randomised, side effects, cost, and a small Hinglish and Hindi list) and there is a current topic | refine; topic = `current.topic` plus the facet words; years kept only when O-13 says so |

It **abstains**, and the model decides, on: refine against new for a short message with a new subject word that partly overlaps; Hindi and Hinglish facets outside the lexicon; any negation; any number other than a year; a pronoun with no current topic; "go back to the first topic" unless exactly one earlier topic matches; a message of ten words or more; anything its closed lists do not name. **A wrong confident decision is worse than a model call** (the prototype made one: "smoking status", the same one today's `needsContext` rule makes), so the default is to abstain.
What a code decision looks like on the reply: "Using your earlier question: {earlier question} / {your message}" (nothing in it is written by a model: the standalone question for the writer is the person's two utterances joined), plus the existing year chip.
What it saves is **in-sample, trust the structure, not the number**: the prototype decided 96 of 113 labelled follow-ups, right on 95, and settled messages that made up 80% of the measured router tokens (127,957 of 159,849); its first clean run was wrong on 8 of 95 decided (8.4%), eight times the 1% it proposed as a gate. That is why a class is switched on only after 30 blind decisions with 0 wrong. The model still does: every abstention; the first-turn router for a message that is not English; and, after R23, the plan call. Mistral Small 3.1 for the follow-up router (to cut about 700 output tokens and a 10 s call) is a spike, not a decision here: nobody has run the follow-up prompt on another model.

### 7.6 Follow-ups after edit, retry, stop and failure

- **Edit and resend:** the new message's context is `foldState` of the path **before** it (the `upTo` semantics), so the replaced text and its replies are not in it (tested: an edited message, then a follow-up).
- **Search again on an old reply:** the turns that existed then, never the later ones; "Use these exact words" and the year chip's remove button are hand-chosen searches with no history (`retryReply`).
- **Stop:** the stopped reply is not a finished search (kept as designed), so a follow-up leans on the search before it; R22 adds one line on the next reply so the person is told instead of surprised. Whether a stopped reply that already showed papers should count as a turn is a question for the chain eval, not a default (the unified and evidence designs changed that rule and its pinned test; this plan does not).
- **Failure** (network, error, interrupted): the same. A router failure on a follow-up: R5.
- **Not turns:** a lane reply, an ask, an id lookup, small talk, another tool's offer, a too-long message (reasons `small_talk`, `safety`, `urgent`, `decision`, `ask`, `lookup`, `routed`, `too_long`, `stopped` are excluded in `historyTurn`; the new ones are added and tested in H0). So a follow-up after a symptom message is a first message with no context.
- **Two tabs and reload:** the fold reads the stored record; R18(a) stops a stale tab from sending.
- **A visitor:** no state is built or sent (unchanged); the resolver is signed-in only because it replaces a model call a visitor never had.

### 7.7 What the person can see and remove

Hidden memory is the risk. Every carried slot is visible and removable in the reply that used it: the year chip, the line "Using your earlier question: ...", the assumption chip ("MS = multiple sclerosis", R17) and the "Left out" line. Carrying years and a requested answer language across follow-ups, **with a visible chip**, is recommended and **off until the owner says yes** (O-13). "New topic" (R22) is the one-press way to stop carrying inside a chat; "Private chat" (exists) keeps a chat out of storage and out of other chats; "Do not use this chat as memory" (exists) concerns the switch. A further "do not use earlier messages in this chat at all" switch is possible (a flag on the chat record that makes `contextBefore` return nothing) but D1 says context is automatic inside a chat, so it is not built (O-14).

### 7.8 One design with the plan, not two

Shared fields: topic words, years, answer language, form, ambiguity (the Reading's `ask`), the "left out" line, the closed word lists, one chips component, one eval schema (section 8: a message plus the prior Readings as state, and a gold Reading), one budget table (3.5), and **two bumps** (R6b, R23) for the whole programme.

### 8.3 The scoring layers

- **L0 per push, no network:** unit and property tests (the cleaner on the trap list; the safety set; `plainText` idempotence; the merge twin case; the fold's property tests); `first-turn.golden.json` (393 entries) and `route-context.test.ts` byte-identical; the 70 English router cases.

- **L2 per push, retrieval replay (recorded answers, seconds):** for each message the lists for three variants, raw (today), read (the product's topic) and oracle (hand-clean), recorded once through the real `runSearch` with the eval's request-URL replay (`eval.eval.ts`; `node eval/patch-fixtures.mjs` adds only the missing recordings; about 750 calls, about 0.75 USD of OpenAlex with the production key, run around 05:30 IST). - **L3 human labels, the gate:** on the hold-out, the top 10 of today's product and of the new one for each message (60 x 10 x 2 = 1,200 labels, about 40 s each) as R (relevant: the topic and, where the message says so, the group or outcome), M (the same subject), I, by one rater with a rubric written in advance, **from the abstract where there is one** and not the title only; a second rater on a random 20%: kappa at least 0.6 on R, M, I and no R-against-I flip, else the labels are not a gate. - **L4 answer level (weekly, real model, signed in):** `apps/api/scripts/chat-eval.mjs` extended by 12 messy messages: no claim without a checked sentence, no written answer over a list with no strong paper, tokens per answer from the ledger, the language of the answer.

### 8.4 The invariants (E5 and its neighbours)

- **E5, byte-identical streams.** With every gate flag at `off` and at `shadow`, the stream of every request of the differential list is byte-identical to the golden (section 4.2, H0), over the 37 requests of `since-year.test.ts` (extended) and about 200 seeded random strings; a throwing, hanging or huge-valued shadow function leaves the stream identical (a 5 ms budget for pure code).
- **Disclosure match** (section 10.2).
- **Canary:** a canary string and 30 hostile strings (entities, newlines, 20,000 characters) appear in no counted value, no D1 bind and no `console` call.
- **Reading-line-true:** every number, year and word in a line the page builds from a Reading is a field of that Reading.
- **State invariants:** the 10 invariants of the evaluation study over 80,000 random steps, plus: the state equals the fold recomputed from a storage round trip; a stale two-tab copy folds to a stale but self-consistent state.
- **Old tab:** an old reducer fed the new events ignores the new fields and shows a new notice kind as plain text.

### 8.5 The gates, written before the first run (file `docs/eval/intent-gates.json`, committed in E0 with its hash in the commit message)

**Gate freeze (replaces "the first run may move a number").** The numbers below are committed before any candidate run. **Only the owner can loosen one, in writing, and never the engineer who missed it.** The blind hold-out is the only honest number and does not exist until E0 ends. Every gate is reported with a bootstrap interval clustered by conversation or case and with a rater-disagreement sensitivity (relevant only against relevant plus marginal: the studies' own R against M calls differed by up to 6 of 10 on comparison and broad-field cases). **Statistics:** with n errors-free cases an upper bound of about 3/n at 95% is all that can be claimed: 0 of 100 shows at most about 3%, 0 of 250 about 1.2%, 0 of 300 about 1%; an observed 2 of 100 shows at most about 7%, 3 of 100 about 8%. Provider answers move run to run (15 and 16 of 20 papers the same on repeats), so replay is the gate and live runs are the drift alarm. **Reading rules, added after the red team.** Every gate states its n beside it and is reported with an interval. A *rate* gate is set and read on the dev sets (the 400 research negatives, the 300 controls, the typo and messy dev sets), and the sealed hold-out is read once, at the end: 20 messages in one language cannot carry a rate (one error in 20 is 5%). A *no worse than baseline* gate is paired and one-sided (no query loses a relevant paper; no more than k right-to-wrong) and not an absolute number copied from the in-sample baseline. A margin is set above the in-sample value and never at it (a limit equal to the in-sample rate is failed about half the time by chance on a fresh set). The sign test on the 113 follow-ups, one-sided: 6 worse and 0 better gives p = 0.016, 6 and 1 gives 0.063, 5 and 1 gives 0.109 [r4-evaluation].

### 10.2 The text is true of the code: the disclosure match, in both directions

- **`apps/api/src/legal-match.test.ts` (new, H0).** It reads `routes.ts` and every file of `apps/api/src/understand/` and asserts that: (1) the string handed to `runSearch` is the reading's topic or the message; (2) the words shown in `understood.reading` equal it; (3) **no module of `understand/` imports a network client, and each is run with `fetch` and the AI binding stubbed to throw** (a grep for imports alone misses a global `fetch()` or `env.AI` call); (4) **each new privacy sentence is pinned to its own flag**: a table `FLAG_SENTENCES` pairs each sentence of 10.1 with the flag whose value in the committed `apps/api/wrangler.jsonc` `vars` block must be `on` (or `core`/`on` for safety) for the sentence to be present, and the test fails in **both** directions: a sentence in `LegalPages.tsx` whose flag is not on in the committed config, or a flag that is on whose sentence is missing.
- **Each text commit goes live at the same deploy that turns its flag on**, never at shadow and never left true after a rollback: a release's go-live is two commits pushed together (the text, then the `vars` line), each reviewable alone, one deploy. A rollback is the revert of that pair, so the repository never says what the code does not. When a gate flag is deleted after 14 clean days its row moves to a second table, `ALWAYS_ON`, whose test asserts that the sentence is present and its code path exists with no flag; a flag deleted without that move fails the test. Releases with no flag (R6b, T1) are `ALWAYS_ON` rows from the start: their sentence is pinned by the code path alone.
- **Known gaps today, all in the safe direction:** (a) the text says Auto sends the beginning of a message to the AI service to choose a tool, although rules settle a simple English one-liner with no model (T1, row 1a, corrects it); (b) the text does not name the outcome counts (`answer_outcomes`, since migration 0019): counts only, no text and no person, which P-COUNTS (row 1) names at R3; (c) between R6a and R6b the text mentions a chip that no longer exists.
- **The one gap, named.** A dashboard rollback of a lane flag during an incident takes effect in a minute, but the sentence "a message that describes an emergency ... is answered by the page itself and sent nowhere" stays on the page until the revert deploys (a few minutes). For those minutes the text over-states. The rule: for a flag whose sentence narrows what is sent (R4, R10, R11), a dashboard rollback is allowed in an incident and is followed within the hour by the revert pair (O-12).
- **The claims table, in every release note:** each changed sentence, the code path that makes it true, the test that pins it; **a sentence with no code path is deleted**.

### 10.3 What is substantive, and why there are two bumps

`PRIVACY_VERSION` (`packages/shared/src/legal.ts:5`, now `2026-11-draft-14`) moves when the change alters **what leaves the device, to whom, or for what purpose, or states a new commitment about memory**; it does not move for a cleaning that sends fewer of the person's own words to the same five sources, for a correction made by our own code inside our own Worker, or for a typo or wording fix (`how-we-work.md` section 5: "a change in what the text promises does"). A bump re-shows the privacy card only before the next upload, Idea Check or scan, never before chatting (`roadmap.md:279`), so it is cheap for people and expensive only as a review.
- **R23 is substantive:** a message that no model reads today would go to the AI service for three named reasons. **Bump.**
- **R6b (P-CHATS) is substantive too:** it states a new commitment about memory (a conversation is one chat; nothing carries into a new chat on its own), which is what D2 asks the text to say and what this section's own definition bumps for. `docs/runbook.md:290` says the text and `PRIVACY_VERSION` change again when the feature goes for good, and `docs/roadmap.md:292` shows the release that introduced the chip and the switch bumped to draft-14. **Bump** (recommended; the owner may read it the other way: O-2). The first draft's "hold the bump and ride R23, never two bumps" is withdrawn: "never two bumps" was this note's rule and not the owner's, and holding a bump means the text changes for weeks under an unchanged version number. Two bumps re-show the card twice, which costs little (only before the next upload, Idea Check or scan).
- **R8 (P-CLEAN) and R14 (P-SPELL)** are on the boundary ("as the words you wrote" is promised today); no new recipient, no new service, no model reads more, and the person sees and can undo it: text only, the owner's call (O-2). Read literally, "alters what leaves the device" also covers a narrowing; this note keeps a narrowing outside the bump because nothing new leaves, and says so to the owner.
- **R21's text** is text only only because the caps stay today's; the unified design's ledger of earlier topics, its model-written standalone question and its shown titles would each be a new use and are not built.
- The two things two designs called "privacy: none" are not: **a Settings line about the Safari limit** and **retiring a router prompt** are text or behaviour changes and go through this same queue if they are ever built.

### 10.4 What is re-run after every text commit, before merge

The pinned-sentence tests (`npx vitest run src/legal-text.test.ts src/legal-match.test.ts`); **the Idea Check checks**: `node scripts/integration.mjs --browser --only="idea check,chat integration,browser: legal pages,browser: chat"` in `apps/api` (suites "idea check integration", "chat integration", "browser: idea check", "browser: idea check in the conversation", "browser: legal pages" and "browser: chat (prompt box)"; the first four matter because the privacy card the version re-shows sits in front of Idea Check and because "Idea Check text never rides along" is pinned by a canary), `npx vitest run src/idea src/chat/context-before.test.ts src/chat/history.test.ts`, `npm run eval` (the prior-art eval); (`apps/web/e2e/legal.mjs`, the page itself, is in that command); and the independent reader's `file:line` per changed sentence. If the other workflow's text or bump lands first, the draft number moves; the test catches a forgotten `RETIRED_VERSIONS` entry (`legal-text.test.ts:37`).

## 11. What is not done, and what is not known

### 11.1 Not done, on purpose

- **Answer forms** (a table, bullet points, length, register). The writer makes one marked paragraph of at most about 120 words (`apps/api/src/chat/answer.ts:19-26`, rendered as one `<p>`), and a table cell written by a model would be unjudged text. The words are left out of the search and the page says so (R8); the follow-up router prompt still tells the model that "bullet points, a table" are a same-papers restyle (`router.ts:130`), a promise the writer cannot keep, which this plan does not fix. A renderer that shows a list only when every item is one claim with its own judge verdict is the guarantee-safe path (unified R12) and is not scheduled.
- **Author, institution, journal and "latest" as provider filters**, a query plan with per-source syntax, OpenAlex `search.semantic` as a lane, a reranker, provider totals ("about 433,000 match, showing 20"). Each needs the cache key to carry the filter, an OpenAlex call-shape experiment and a plan object only R23 can fill; the 27 Oct 429 storm is the precedent against fan-out. The notes of R8 only say so honestly.
- **PubMed abstracts** (production PubMed returns none: `pubmed.ts:50`; an efetch per list gave abstracts for 96 to 98% of records in the providers study). The highest-value data fix not scheduled; it would give the writer more papers and the tiers real abstracts. Recommended next.
- **Abuse, violence, a child in danger** and any other emergency that is not a symptom of the sender or of a person the sender describes: not in R4's first wave (O-16); those messages are searched like any other, and this note says so plainly.
- **Hindi and Hinglish for visitors** (no model; searched as written: probe-b strict precision at 10 of 2%). Options are O-7, a first-priority owner decision; if none is chosen, the biggest measured failure for the Indian audience stays.
- **Rewriting the follow-up router's prompt to read the state:** R21 keeps sending `history` for the router, and a prompt that read the state would be a separate release with the follow-up eval.
- **A public speller, ever** (a guarantee); a model-written summary or memory; a server copy of a chat; history for the judge, Check strictly or the background call.
- **The Settings switch "Use my earlier chats"** (O-1); a ledger of more than three earlier topics on the wire.
- **A networked shadow search of real messages** (P13); **press counters** and a reversal tripwire (O-5).
- **Deleting the three private cleaners**, the dead pieces of the history code (`MAX_HISTORY_QUERY_CHARS`, `ChatContextRequest`, `HistoryEntry.language` sent and never read, a duplicated history reader with a retry-without-history hack) and the duplicated `ruleRoute` calls: a tidy release after the counters are clean.
- **A model flag for the ask rule** (D2 of the staged design): only if a spike (OpenAlex `group_by` on the primary topic of the top results, untested) finds a source of index-verified options.
- **Mistral Small 3.1 for the follow-up router**, prefix caching with `x-session-affinity` (a stable id would reach Cloudflare and must stay out of every log; the gain is unmeasured), a thinner router prompt: spikes in the evaluation track.
- **Searching a fragment of a long paste by itself** (R15 offers a button instead) and **searching the topic automatically after a refusal** (R10 offers a button instead): both need a text change and the owner's word (O-9).

### 11.2 Not known

- **No model has been run on any of this**: not the router on a messy message (`search_query` for a polite or typo message is unknown), not a plan call (its 525 to 1,700 extra tokens, its latency and its false-correction rate), not the writer, not the judge; whether gpt-oss-120b is subject to the 20-requests-a-minute per-model cap that Cloudflare's limits page states for models that need the paid plan (the page does not name our models), and whether a Workers AI binding call counts toward the 10,000 subrequests. **What a signed-in person gets for the owner's message was never seen.**
- **In-sample or confounded numbers (section 1):** the frame prototype, the tier fractions, the 98% ladder, the validator's 40 of 40, the coverage AUC of 0.94 (from a regex judge), the resolver's 85% and 99%; about 235 to 246 probes from one IP in 20 minutes; four precision baselines on four rubrics; all 185 cases written after the owner's example. The blind hold-out is the only honest number and does not exist yet.
- **The helpline numbers** (112, Tele-MANAS 14416) and **every Hinglish and Hindi sentence** (not native-read): both are preconditions of `CHAT_SAFETY=core` (4.5).
- **Times that nobody measured:** the time to the first `papers` event of a search (R14's gate needs it), and the code time of the speller and of the safety scan.
- **The word list:** its licence, its size, its coverage outside computing and biomedicine, the damage it does to valid rare words, the time and memory of a 60,000-word search inside a Worker (the providers study timed 2.9 ms a token on 4,477 words).
- **`navigator.storage.persist()`:** that Firefox prompts and Chromium grants silently, what Safari does; `fake-indexeddb` against the real `blocked` and `versionchange` events.
- **OpenAlex with the production key:** `meta.count`, filters, `search.semantic`, and whether every arXiv number is found by its DOI (every study's keyless allowance of $0.10 a day was spent, HTTP 429 after about 25 calls); Semantic Scholar with messy text (429 on 15 of 15 calls).
- **The plan and billing:** Workers Paid is from `docs/roadmap.md`, not seen on the dashboard; the free-neuron account limit (10,000 a day); whether `wrangler rollback` works on this account (the rollback relied on is the flag).
- **The real shares:** how often real messages are messy, carry ids, look like a symptom, or are answered by one provider only. No counter exists; R3 is the first release for that reason.
- **Real phone, Safari, a screen reader.** DPDP and other law (not read; the studies' sources conflict on dates); a lawyer reads the queue.

### 11.3 The risks, largest first

1. A lane rule that fires on real research (a researcher of suicide prevention; a user of weather or recipe research). Mitigation: intent phrases rather than the word, first-person and now-markers, one-press ways out except for self-harm, 260 and 400 negatives, `core` first, the tripwire, shadow counts.
1a. The opposite error: a lane that misses real distress (a long message, "suicidal" wording, "I need help", someone else in danger), which the gate cannot see because its positives are written by people, and a person in trouble who is searched is the worst outcome in the note. Mitigation: scenarios written first by a clinician and a student, the whole message read, the families named by the red team in the dev set and the sealed hold-out, HELP before small talk, and the plain statement (11.1, O-16) of what is not covered.
2. The frame cleaner changing a title-like message ("What Is Life?"). Mitigation: the two-content-word guard, the visible reading line, "Use my exact words".
3. The floor hiding a relevant paper from the writer or announcing "no close match" wrongly. Mitigation: three tiers (fold only the weak), the trust rule (1 to 6 content words), the background answer still runs, nothing deleted, `writer` mode first.
4. A wrong spelling correction. Mitigation: suggest first; apply only when a second search confirms it; protected tokens; never hand-chosen; the latency cap.
5. The `routes.ts` and `ChatView.tsx` merges against the other workflow (9.2). Mitigation: Phase 0 pure modules, one-call hooks, one holder per HIGH file.
6. The first-load budget (248.7 of 250 KB at HEAD; their diet may help): R17 and the fold add bytes; R6a removes some; the limits are not raised.
7. Provider cost and lanes: a repair search doubles calls for the few flagged messages; unique strings always miss the cache; abuse costs about 0.06 to 0.12 USD a minute from one IP at the limit. Mitigation: a sequential second search, flagged messages only, the existing 60-a-minute limiter, `plainText` on read instead of a cache-key bump.
8. The safety sentences are wrong or the numbers out of date. Mitigation: sign-offs, the numbers in one constant that is null until checked, the flag.
9. A privacy text that is not true of the code. Mitigation: 10.2 in both directions and the one named gap.
10. Too many flags. Mitigation: at most twelve at once (the kill switches `CHAT_SAFETY` and `CHAT_PLAN` counted apart), the combination test (all off, all shadow, each alone on, all on, and the pairs that share a path: clean with floor, clean with spell, floor with spell, safety with talk, ids with clean), and the rule that a gate flag is deleted after 14 clean days.

11. Releases with no switch (R6a, R6b, R7, R18's fixes, R19, R22): a rollback is a revert in about 15 to 20 minutes. R18(a), the only one that can stop a message from being sent, has its own server switch and fails open.
12. The first-load style sheet has 1.3 KB left (24.7 of 26 KB). Mitigation: new chat styles in the lazy `ChatView` chunk's stylesheet, a budget line in every web release, R6a removes `.hm-menu-list` and `.hm-more`.
